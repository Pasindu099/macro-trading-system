1. Net Positioning ChartEUR

Speculative Net Position shows how many contracts large hedge funds and money managers are long versus short. When the bar is deeply negative, specs are heavily short - this is often a contrarian bullish signal because crowded shorts unwind fast. When deeply positive, specs are heavily long - watch for potential reversals. The key insight is not the absolute number but the direction of change week over week.

2. Extremes Index52-week range

The Extremes Index (COT Index) ranks current speculative positioning against the past 52 weeks as a percentile. Above 80 means specs are more long than 80% of the past year - historically a warning that the long trade is crowded and a reversal is likely. Below 20 means specs are historically very short - a potential contrarian long opportunity. Professional traders use the 80/20 levels as high-conviction reversal zones, not entry signals on their own.

3. Commercial vs Speculator SpreadNet gap

Commercials are businesses hedging real exposure - exporters, importers, central banks. They are always on the opposite side of speculators and they are considered the smart money. When the gap between commercial net and speculator net reaches an extreme, it means specs are maximally positioned against the smart money. Historically these extreme gaps precede spec capitulation and sharp price reversals. Watch for the lines to converge after a wide gap.

4. Open Interest TrendWeekly participation

Open Interest is the total number of outstanding contracts. Rising open interest with rising price confirms a strong trend - new money is entering. Rising open interest with falling price confirms a strong downtrend. Falling open interest means traders are closing positions - the trend may be exhausting. A sudden spike in open interest often marks a turning point as one side gets caught.

5. Weekly Positioning DeltaSpec net WoW

Weekly Positioning Delta shows how much specs changed their net position in a single week. Large sudden changes are more significant than gradual moves. A large negative delta (rapid short increase) after a long period of longs suggests a sentiment shift - institutional money is turning bearish. Three consecutive weeks of the same direction delta is a momentum confirmation.

6. Divergence Detector8-week direction

Divergence occurs when price makes a new high or low but speculative positioning does not confirm it. This is one of the highest-conviction reversal signals in the COT framework. If price makes a new high but specs are reducing longs - distribution is happening. Smart money is selling into retail buying. Divergences do not give you a precise entry but they tell you the trend is weakening before price confirms it.

7. Multi-Currency Relative StrengthAll major FX futures

The Multi-Currency Relative Strength table shows all currencies simultaneously ranked by their COT Index percentile. Use this to identify pairs with maximum divergence - for example if AUD is at the 5th percentile (extremely short) and USD is at the 90th percentile (extremely long), that is a high conviction SHORT AUD/USD setup from a positioning perspective. Always combine with fundamental and trend analysis.

8. USD Composite Index7-contract aggregate

The USD Composite Index aggregates speculative positioning across all USD pairs to give a single view of how institutions are positioned on the dollar overall. When this index is at an extreme it affects all USD pairs simultaneously. A deeply negative USD composite means specs are very short dollars across the board - a potential dollar squeeze is building. Use this as a macro filter before trading any USD pair.

COT Pair Differential Matrix Spec positioning differential per pair

The differential is calculated by subtracting the quote currency COT Index percentile from the base currency percentile. A large positive number means specs are much more long the base than the quote - a positioning-based bullish signal for the pair. A large negative number means specs favour the quote currency. Values beyond +40 or -40 represent high-conviction positioning. The Commercial differential shows what the smart money hedgers are doing - they are always opposite to specs and are considered more reliable at extremes.

Squeeze Risk Monitor Pairs at risk of spec capitulation

Squeeze risk is highest when specs are extremely positioned on both legs of a pair simultaneously. If specs are at the 5th percentile on AUD and at the 95th percentile on USD, any catalyst can trigger a violent reversal as both sides unwind at once. High squeeze risk does not mean the trade is wrong - but it means the reversal when it comes will be fast. Size down on high squeeze risk pairs.

Commercial vs Spec Alignment Smart money vs crowd by pair

When commercials and speculators are on opposite extremes for both currencies in a pair, the setup is considered highest conviction. Commercials are hedging real business - they rarely get this wrong at extremes. When they are maximally long a currency that specs are maximally short, watch for a trend reversal. Green alignment means commercials support the spec direction. Red means commercials are fighting the spec trend - historically a warning sign.
